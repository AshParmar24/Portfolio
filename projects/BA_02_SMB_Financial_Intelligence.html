SMB Operational and Financial Intelligence

Client scenario. Harlow & Finch is a three-depot trade wholesaler in the East Midlands turning over about £8.8 million. The owners could see sales rising and profit falling, but their accounts package only gave them a monthly total. They asked three questions: where is the margin going, why is cash tight, and is anything in operations costing us customers?

My role. I delivered this as a Datavra engagement: agreed the KPIs with the owners, documented every data field, built the data model and SQL, produced the dashboard and wrote up the root causes with a value on each.

What I found

FindingEvidenceValue
1. Timber costs rose and prices did notTimber unit cost stepped up 13 percent in February 2026 while its list price index stayed at 100. Timber margin fell from 23.2 to 14.0 percent; every other category moved by less than one point.£168,129 of gross profit lost in FY26, about £224,172 a year at the current rate
2. Discount creep at one depotTwo Nottingham reps moved from about 7.8 percent to over 13 percent average discount on credit accounts. The other seven reps did not move.£78,752 given away above their own FY25 level
3. Largest trade accounts are paying laterTrade accounts took 39 days to pay in FY25 and 51 days by summer 2026 on 30 day terms. All 15 of the largest accounts slowed down.About £223,702 more cash tied up in debtors; £511,857 of £1,250,526 owed is overdue
4. Derby depot is dispatching lateOn time in full at Derby fell from 90 percent to 78 percent from June 2026 (67 percent in October). Leicester and Nottingham held at 90 percent.Service risk on the depot's credit accounts

The gross profit bridge reconciles FY25 to FY26 to the pound: supplier cost took £228,437, discounting £87,957, and the April list price rise on non-timber lines gave back £90,434. Volume and mix together were worth £29,602. Gross profit fell by £196,359 in total.

What I recommended

  • Reprice timber now, then link price reviews to supplier cost changes. Each 1 percent on timber list price is worth about £22,205 a year at current volumes. Add a monthly cost against price check by category to the dashboard so a gap shows within a month, not a year.
  • Put a discount approval limit in the order system. Anything above 10 percent on a credit account needs depot manager sign-off, and discount by rep becomes a standing KPI.
  • Start credit control at 30 days for the top 40 accounts. Statement on day 31, call on day 38, account on stop at day 50. Target: back to 40 days.
  • Investigate dispatch capacity at Derby. The data shows when the problem started but not why. Next step is a site visit and a look at picking rotas and vehicle availability for June onwards.

SQL behind the dashboard

Each query is named, and the analysis script runs them in order and saves one CSV per query. Database: SQLite.

kpi_headline_by_year.sql
-- Headline KPIs for each financial year: revenue, gross profit, gross margin and average discount.
WITH sales AS (
    SELECT CASE WHEN o.order_date < '2025-11-01' THEN 'FY25' ELSE 'FY26' END AS fin_year,
           l.quantity * l.list_price AS list_value,
           l.net_revenue,
           l.cost_of_sales
    FROM fact_order_lines l
    JOIN fact_orders o ON o.order_id = l.order_id
)
SELECT fin_year,
       ROUND(SUM(net_revenue))                                              AS revenue,
       ROUND(SUM(net_revenue) - SUM(cost_of_sales))                         AS gross_profit,
       ROUND(100.0 * (SUM(net_revenue) - SUM(cost_of_sales)) / SUM(net_revenue), 2) AS gross_margin_pct,
       ROUND(100.0 * (1 - SUM(net_revenue) / SUM(list_value)), 2)           AS avg_discount_pct
FROM sales
GROUP BY fin_year;
sales_cube_monthly.sql
-- Monthly sales by depot, customer segment and product category. This feeds the dashboard filters.
SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', o.order_date)          AS month,
       d.depot_name                             AS depot,
       c.segment,
       p.category,
       ROUND(SUM(l.quantity * l.list_price), 2) AS list_value,
       ROUND(SUM(l.net_revenue), 2)             AS revenue,
       ROUND(SUM(l.cost_of_sales), 2)           AS cost_of_sales
FROM fact_order_lines l
JOIN fact_orders o   ON o.order_id = l.order_id
JOIN dim_customer c  ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
JOIN dim_depot d     ON d.depot_id = o.depot_id
JOIN dim_product p   ON p.product_id = l.product_id
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3, 4;
margin_by_category_year.sql
-- Where did margin move? Gross margin by category, FY25 against FY26, with the change in points.
WITH s AS (
    SELECT p.category,
           CASE WHEN o.order_date < '2025-11-01' THEN 'FY25' ELSE 'FY26' END AS fin_year,
           SUM(l.net_revenue) AS revenue,
           SUM(l.net_revenue) - SUM(l.cost_of_sales) AS gross_profit
    FROM fact_order_lines l
    JOIN fact_orders o ON o.order_id = l.order_id
    JOIN dim_product p ON p.product_id = l.product_id
    GROUP BY 1, 2
)
SELECT category,
       ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY25' THEN revenue END))                       AS revenue_fy25,
       ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN revenue END))                       AS revenue_fy26,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY25' THEN gross_profit END)
                   / SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY25' THEN revenue END), 2)            AS margin_pct_fy25,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN gross_profit END)
                   / SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN revenue END), 2)            AS margin_pct_fy26,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN gross_profit END)
                   / SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN revenue END)
           - 100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY25' THEN gross_profit END)
                   / SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY25' THEN revenue END), 2)            AS change_pts
FROM s
GROUP BY category
ORDER BY change_pts;
timber_cost_vs_price.sql
-- Timber: average unit cost and average list price per month, indexed to November 2024 = 100.
-- A cost line that climbs while the price line stays flat is a pricing gap, not a sales problem.
WITH m AS (
    SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', o.order_date) AS month,
           SUM(l.quantity * l.unit_cost)  / SUM(l.quantity * p.base_unit_cost) AS cost_ratio,
           SUM(l.quantity * l.list_price) / SUM(l.quantity * p.list_price)     AS price_ratio
    FROM fact_order_lines l
    JOIN fact_orders o ON o.order_id = l.order_id
    JOIN dim_product p ON p.product_id = l.product_id
    WHERE p.category = 'Timber & Sheet'
    GROUP BY 1
)
SELECT month,
       ROUND(100.0 * cost_ratio, 1)  AS unit_cost_index,
       ROUND(100.0 * price_ratio, 1) AS list_price_index
FROM m
ORDER BY month;
discount_by_rep_year.sql
-- Average discount given by each sales rep on credit accounts, FY25 against FY26, ranked by the increase.
WITH s AS (
    SELECT r.rep_name, d.depot_name AS depot,
           CASE WHEN o.order_date < '2025-11-01' THEN 'FY25' ELSE 'FY26' END AS fin_year,
           SUM(l.quantity * l.list_price) AS list_value,
           SUM(l.net_revenue)             AS revenue
    FROM fact_order_lines l
    JOIN fact_orders o  ON o.order_id = l.order_id
    JOIN dim_customer c ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
    JOIN dim_rep r      ON r.rep_id = o.rep_id
    JOIN dim_depot d    ON d.depot_id = r.depot_id
    WHERE c.segment <> 'Cash & Retail'
    GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
)
SELECT rep_name, depot,
       ROUND(100.0 * (1 - SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY25' THEN revenue END)
                        / SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY25' THEN list_value END)), 2) AS discount_pct_fy25,
       ROUND(100.0 * (1 - SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN revenue END)
                        / SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN list_value END)), 2) AS discount_pct_fy26,
       ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN list_value END))                  AS list_value_fy26
FROM s
GROUP BY rep_name, depot
ORDER BY discount_pct_fy26 - discount_pct_fy25 DESC;
discount_trend_flagged_reps.sql
-- Monthly discount for the two flagged reps against everyone else (credit accounts only).
SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', o.order_date) AS month,
       CASE WHEN r.rep_name IN ('E. Purcell', 'F. Sandhu') THEN 'Purcell and Sandhu' ELSE 'All other reps' END AS rep_group,
       ROUND(100.0 * (1 - SUM(l.net_revenue) / SUM(l.quantity * l.list_price)), 2) AS discount_pct
FROM fact_order_lines l
JOIN fact_orders o  ON o.order_id = l.order_id
JOIN dim_customer c ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
JOIN dim_rep r      ON r.rep_id = o.rep_id
WHERE c.segment <> 'Cash & Retail'
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY 1, 2;
days_to_pay_monthly.sql
-- Cash collection: value-weighted average days from invoice to payment, by invoice month and segment.
-- Credit customers only. Unpaid invoices are excluded, so the latest two months understate the true figure.
SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', i.invoice_date) AS month,
       c.segment,
       ROUND(SUM(i.amount * (julianday(i.paid_date) - julianday(i.invoice_date))) / SUM(i.amount), 1) AS avg_days_to_pay,
       ROUND(SUM(i.amount)) AS invoiced_value
FROM fact_invoices i
JOIN fact_orders o  ON o.order_id = i.order_id
JOIN dim_customer c ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
WHERE c.segment <> 'Cash & Retail'
  AND i.paid_date IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY 1, 2;
aged_debt_snapshot.sql
-- Aged debt at the reporting date (31 October 2026): what is owed, and how overdue it is.
WITH open_items AS (
    SELECT c.segment, i.amount,
           julianday('2026-10-31') - julianday(i.due_date) AS days_overdue
    FROM fact_invoices i
    JOIN fact_orders o  ON o.order_id = i.order_id
    JOIN dim_customer c ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
    WHERE i.paid_date IS NULL
)
SELECT segment,
       ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN days_overdue <= 0 THEN amount ELSE 0 END))               AS not_yet_due,
       ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN days_overdue BETWEEN 1 AND 30 THEN amount ELSE 0 END))   AS overdue_1_30,
       ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN days_overdue > 30 THEN amount ELSE 0 END))               AS overdue_over_30,
       ROUND(SUM(amount))                                                           AS total_owed
FROM open_items
GROUP BY segment;
late_payers_top_accounts.sql
-- Which accounts drive the slowdown? Largest trade accounts, days to pay FY25 against FY26.
WITH p AS (
    SELECT o.customer_id,
           CASE WHEN i.invoice_date < '2025-11-01' THEN 'FY25' ELSE 'FY26' END AS fin_year,
           SUM(i.amount) AS invoiced,
           SUM(i.amount * (julianday(i.paid_date) - julianday(i.invoice_date))) / SUM(i.amount) AS days_to_pay
    FROM fact_invoices i
    JOIN fact_orders o  ON o.order_id = i.order_id
    JOIN dim_customer c ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
    WHERE c.segment = 'Trade Account' AND i.paid_date IS NOT NULL AND i.invoice_date < '2026-09-01'
    GROUP BY 1, 2
)
SELECT customer_id,
       ROUND(SUM(invoiced))                                             AS invoiced_two_years,
       ROUND(MAX(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY25' THEN days_to_pay END), 1)  AS days_to_pay_fy25,
       ROUND(MAX(CASE WHEN fin_year = 'FY26' THEN days_to_pay END), 1)  AS days_to_pay_fy26
FROM p
GROUP BY customer_id
ORDER BY invoiced_two_years DESC
LIMIT 15;
otif_monthly_by_depot.sql
-- Operations: on time in full (OTIF) by depot and month.
-- A delivery counts only if it was dispatched on or before the promised date and was complete.
SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', o.order_date) AS month,
       d.depot_name AS depot,
       COUNT(*) AS deliveries,
       SUM(CASE WHEN f.dispatched_date <= f.promised_date AND f.complete_flag = 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS on_time_in_full,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN f.dispatched_date <= f.promised_date AND f.complete_flag = 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*), 1) AS otif_pct
FROM fact_deliveries f
JOIN fact_orders o ON o.order_id = f.order_id
JOIN dim_depot d   ON d.depot_id = f.depot_id
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY 1, 2;

Gross profit bridge (Python)

The change in unit margin for each product splits exactly into list price, discount and cost. The script checks that the steps add back to FY26 gross profit.

ba02_analysis.py (extract)
# ---- 2. gross profit bridge at product level
lines = pd.read_sql("""
    SELECT l.product_id, p.category, o.rep_id,
           CASE WHEN o.order_date < '2025-11-01' THEN 'FY25' ELSE 'FY26' END AS fy,
           l.quantity AS q, l.quantity * l.list_price AS list_value,
           l.net_revenue AS rev, l.cost_of_sales AS cos
    FROM fact_order_lines l
    JOIN fact_orders o ON o.order_id = l.order_id
    JOIN dim_product p ON p.product_id = l.product_id""", con)

g = lines.groupby(["product_id", "category", "fy"])[["q", "list_value", "rev", "cos"]].sum().unstack("fy")
g.columns = [f"{a}_{b}" for a, b in g.columns]
g = g.reset_index()
for y in ("FY25", "FY26"):
    g[f"list_{y}"] = g[f"list_value_{y}"] / g[f"q_{y}"]          # average list price per unit
    g[f"disc_{y}"] = (g[f"list_value_{y}"] - g[f"rev_{y}"]) / g[f"q_{y}"]   # discount given per unit
    g[f"cost_{y}"] = g[f"cos_{y}"] / g[f"q_{y}"]                 # cost per unit
    g[f"margin_{y}"] = g[f"list_{y}"] - g[f"disc_{y}"] - g[f"cost_{y}"]

gp25 = (g.rev_FY25 - g.cos_FY25).sum()
gp26 = (g.rev_FY26 - g.cos_FY26).sum()
# Unit margin change splits exactly into list price, discount and cost. Each is valued at FY26 volumes.
price_fx = (g.q_FY26 * (g.list_FY26 - g.list_FY25)).sum()
disc_fx = -(g.q_FY26 * (g.disc_FY26 - g.disc_FY25)).sum()
cost_fx = -(g.q_FY26 * (g.cost_FY26 - g.cost_FY25)).sum()
vol_mix = ((g.q_FY26 - g.q_FY25) * g.margin_FY25).sum()
# Volume is growth at constant FY25 net prices applied to FY25 profit. Mix is what is left of volume and mix.
net25 = g.rev_FY25 / g.q_FY25
volume_fx = gp25 * ((g.q_FY26 * net25).sum() / g.rev_FY25.sum() - 1)
mix_fx = vol_mix - volume_fx
bridge = [("FY25 gross profit", gp25), ("Volume", volume_fx), ("Product mix", mix_fx), ("List price", price_fx),
          ("Discounting", disc_fx), ("Supplier cost", cost_fx), ("FY26 gross profit", gp26)]
assert abs(gp25 + volume_fx + mix_fx + price_fx + disc_fx + cost_fx - gp26) < 1, "bridge does not reconcile"
