Enquiry to Booking Process Redesign

Client scenario. Wren & Harbour Travel is an independent agency with two branches and 11 consultants, selling package and tailor-made holidays. Enquiries arrive by phone, email, web form, walk-in and social media, and each consultant tracks their own in a personal spreadsheet. The managing director believes enquiries are being lost but cannot see where.

My role. Business analyst. I measured the current process from an eight-week enquiry log, mapped it, agreed objectives with the sponsor, and produced the requirements a new booking system has to meet, written so that a supplier could be assessed against them.

Problem statement

Written enquiries wait in a shared inbox, quotes are not chased, and customer details are typed three times before a booking is confirmed. The agency converts 32 percent of enquiries answered within two hours and 13 percent of those that wait more than eight; 36 percent of quotes that are followed up and 16 percent of those that are not. 10 percent of bookings need a correction, and consultants spend about 46 minutes of admin on each enquiry.

Expected benefit

If follow-up rises to 95 percent and written enquiries are answered within the target, and only a third of each observed conversion gap is counted, the model gives about 26 extra bookings in eight weeks: roughly £740,975 of booking value and £81,507 of commission a year at an assumed 11 percent margin.

I have been careful with this number. The conversion gaps in the log are associations: customers who get a fast reply may differ from those who do not. The one-third prudence factor is an input cell in the workbook, and my recommendation is a four-week pilot of the two-hour reply rule in one branch before the saving is put in a business case.
