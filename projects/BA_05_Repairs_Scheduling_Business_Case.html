Business Case: Repairs Scheduling and Tenant Self-Service

Context. This follows directly from my repairs service review. That analysis found 3,166 no-access visits and 4,420 return visits in 12 months, and a damp and mould process that cannot evidence the legal investigation timescale. The board asked: should we buy a scheduling system, build one, or fix the process without new software?

My role. Business analyst. I defined the options, built the financial model in Excel with live formulas, scored the non-financial criteria with stakeholders' priorities, tested the sensitivity and wrote the recommendation.

Recommendation

Do Option 1 now. Take Option 2 to procurement only if the board accepts that it is a compliance and service investment, not a savings one.

  • Option 1 wins on money. Text reminders and a contractor improvement plan cost £19,800 to set up, pay back in year 1 and return 4.7 times their cost. They still pay back if only 21 percent of the planned benefits arrive.
  • Option 2 wins on everything else. It is the only option that gives date-stamped evidence for the damp and mould timescales and lets tenants choose their own slot. Its NPV is positive at £150,412, but it needs 75 percent of planned benefits to break even, and it is £75,509 worse than Option 1 over five years.
  • So the real question for the board is this: is compliance evidence and a better tenant experience worth about £15,102 a year more than the cheaper route? I set that out as the decision, and did not present Option 2 as the financial winner, because it is not.
  • Option 3 should be rejected. A bespoke build does not pay back within five years (NPV (£155,243)) and scores lowest on delivery risk.

Where the benefits come from

Each benefit pool is measured from the repairs data, and each option captures only a share of it.

Assumptions and limits

Volumes are measured from the repairs dataset. Supplier, build and staff costs are illustrative figures chosen for this case study and would be replaced by quotations in a live case. The discount rate is the 3.5 percent standard rate from the HM Treasury Green Book. Every input is a labelled blue cell in the workbook, so the model can be re-run with different figures in seconds.

Annual benefit for an option (Options sheet)
=Baseline!$B$13*D11+Baseline!$B$14*D12+Baseline!$B$15*D13

each pool x the share that option captures
Discount factor and net present value (Cash flows sheet)
Discount factor, year t:   =1/(1+Assumptions!$B$5)^t
Present value of costs:    =SUMPRODUCT(one_off_row+recurring_row, discount_factor_row)
Present value of benefits: =SUMPRODUCT(benefits_row, discount_factor_row)
Net present value:         =SUM(present_value_of_net_cash_flow_row)
Payback year (Cash flows sheet)
Marker for each year:  =IF(AND(cumulative_this_year>=0, cumulative_last_year<0), year, "")
Payback:               =IF(COUNT(marker_row)=0, "Beyond year 5", MIN(marker_row))

The workbook recalculates with zero formula errors. Blue cells on yellow are inputs; everything else is a formula.
